## Design Context

### Users

Clinical, CME, quality, and compliance reviewers working through high-stakes source changes; professionals completing short probes and artifact corrections; auditors verifying observable process evidence. The primary job is to decide what a changed rule affects, authorize the right remediation, and prove what happened without overstating what the system measured.

### Brand Personality

Calm, exacting, and dignified. Supersede should feel like a well-made evidence ledger used by serious experts, not a generic analytics dashboard or an AI assistant performing confidence theater.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light, restrained editorial workspace: warm ivory surfaces, aubergine ink and accent, quiet clinical status colors, strong serif display typography paired with a highly readable sans face, thin rules, and deliberate density. The approved Graph Briefing, Evidence Ledger, and Change Room concepts guide the system. Avoid gradients, glass effects, nested card grids, decorative charts, neon AI styling, and empty visual flourish.

### Design Principles

1. Put evidence and complete dependency paths before summary metrics.
2. Make uncertainty, approval state, version lock, and actor responsibility visible at the decision point.
3. Use observable language: potential exposure, assigned, assessed, remediated, and rechecked.
4. Keep learner work cold, short, mobile-first, and respectful.
5. Adapt the workflow for mobile rather than hiding critical actions or shrinking the desktop layout.
